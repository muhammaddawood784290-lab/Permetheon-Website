# 30 — DECISION DEADLINES & ESCALATION TIMES

The project must use explicit decision deadlines and non-overlapping escalation rules.

Decision urgency is determined by **delivery impact**, not by who requested the decision.

Every decision must have:

* Decision ID
* Priority
* Decision owner
* Date/time requested
* Decision required
* Impact if delayed
* Decision deadline
* First escalation time
* Final escalation time
* Final escalation owner
* Current status

---

# DECISION PRIORITY MODEL

The decision classes are intentionally separated by urgency.

| Priority | Meaning                      | Decision Window | Escalation Model        |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | --------------: | ----------------------- |
| D0       | Launch / production critical |         4 hours | Hour-level escalation   |
| D1       | Current phase blocking       |  1 business day | Business-day escalation |
| D2       | Non-blocking                 | 2 business days | Daily escalation        |
| D3       | Polish / optional            | 3 business days | Backlog-first           |

**D0 and D1 must never use the same escalation schedule.**

---

# D0 — CRITICAL / LAUNCH DECISION

D0 is reserved ONLY for decisions that can prevent a safe production launch or cause a critical production failure.

## Examples

* Production launch approval
* Domain ownership/configuration
* Critical production configuration
* Critical contact destination
* Critical security issue
* Critical legal/business information
* Production environment approval
* Critical factual correction discovered immediately before launch

D0 must NOT be used for:

* Normal design feedback
* Homepage copy preference
* Case-study wording
* Optional features
* Minor UI decisions
* General business preferences

---

## D0 TIMELINE

### T+0 — Decision requested

Create the decision record and notify the owner.

### T+1 hour — First reminder

If unresolved:

Send a concise reminder.

Status:

**APPROACHING DEADLINE**

### T+2 hours — Critical escalation

If still unresolved:

Escalate immediately to:

**Final Project Authority / Permetheon Business Owner**

Status:

**ESCALATED — D0**

### T+4 hours — Final deadline

If still unresolved:

Status:

**BLOCKED — LAUNCH DECISION REQUIRED**

Action:

**STOP affected launch activity.**

Do not make an irreversible assumption.

---

## D0 ESCALATION MODEL

```text
T+0
Decision Requested
    ↓
T+1h
Reminder
    ↓
T+2h
Critical Escalation
    ↓
T+4h
Final Deadline
    ↓
STOP / RESOLVE
```

D0 decisions therefore operate entirely inside a **4-hour emergency window**.

---

# D1 — PHASE-BLOCKING DECISION

D1 applies when the current implementation phase cannot pass its gate without a decision, but the issue does NOT represent an immediate production/launch risk.

## Examples

* Homepage positioning approval
* Design-system direction
* Project feature verification
* Required case-study information
* Project screenshot approval
* Project category
* Service structure
* Major navigation decision
* Case-study content approval

---

# D1 TIMELINE

D1 intentionally uses a slower timeline than D0.

### T+0 — Decision requested

Create the decision record.

### T+4 business hours — First reminder

If unresolved:

Status:

**APPROACHING D1 DEADLINE**

Send a reminder containing:

* Decision required
* Impact
* Deadline
* Options if applicable

### T+1 business day — Primary deadline

If unresolved:

Status:

**OVERDUE — D1**

Escalate to the designated backup or higher decision owner.

### T+2 business days — Final escalation

If still unresolved:

Status:

**PHASE BLOCKED — D1 ESCALATION**

Pause only the dependent work.

Continue all independent implementation.

The affected phase cannot pass its gate until the decision is resolved.

---

## D1 ESCALATION MODEL

```text
T+0
Decision Requested
    ↓
T+4 business hours
Reminder
    ↓
T+1 business day
Primary Deadline + Escalation
    ↓
T+2 business days
Final Escalation
    ↓
Dependent Phase Remains Blocked
```

D1 therefore operates on a **business-day timeline**, clearly separated from D0's emergency timeline.

---

# D0 VS D1 — HARD DISTINCTION

Use this rule when deciding between D0 and D1:

### Ask:

**"Can the website safely continue toward production without this decision?"**

If **NO because launch/safety/production is immediately affected:**

→ **D0**

If **NO because the current phase cannot complete, but production is not immediately at risk:**

→ **D1**

If **YES and independent work can continue:**

→ **D2 or D3**

# IMPORTANT TIME RULE

Use the user's/project team's agreed working timezone for all deadlines.

For business-day decisions:

* Count only agreed working hours.
* Do not treat weekends/non-working days as business hours.
* If a decision is requested outside working hours, the decision clock begins at the next working period unless explicitly marked D0.

For D0:

The emergency clock begins immediately if the team has explicitly designated the issue as a production/launch emergency.

---

# NO ESCALATION OVERLAP RULE

Never assign the same escalation deadline to different decision classes simply because they are convenient.

The hierarchy must remain:

**D0 = hours**

**D1 = business days**

**D2 = days**

**D3 = backlog**

This guarantees that a production-critical decision is visibly more urgent than a phase-level decision.

Every decision must have:

**Priority**
→
**Owner**
→
**Deadline**
→
**Escalation**
→
**Resolution**
→
**Verification**

A decision cannot remain indefinitely in:

**"Waiting for approval."**

Instead it must move through:

**OPEN**
→
**APPROACHING DEADLINE**
→
**ESCALATED**
→
**RESOLVED**
→
**VERIFIED**

or, where appropriate:

**OPEN**
→
**DEFERRED**
→
**BACKLOG**

The goal is not to rush every decision.

The goal is to make the urgency and consequences of every decision explicit.

# DECISION RECORD RULES

## 1. One decision must answer one question

Avoid vague records such as:

"Need approval on website."

Instead:

"Should the primary navigation use Work, Services, Process, About and Start a Project?"

A decision record must make the required decision immediately understandable.

---

## 2. Record the decision, not the entire discussion

Do not copy long conversations into the decision record.

Capture:

* What was decided
* Why
* Who decided
* When
* What changes
* Who implements it
* How it will be verified

The record should remain useful weeks later.

---

## 3. Every decision must have an owner

No decision may remain:

"Waiting for approval."

Instead identify the person or role responsible for making the decision.

Example:

**Decision Owner:** Permetheon / Business Owner

---

## 4. Every decision must have a deadline

Use the priority model from Section 30.

| Priority | Decision Deadline | Purpose                      |
| -------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------- |
| D0       | 4 hours           | Production / launch critical |
| D1       | 1 business day    | Current phase blocking       |
| D2       | 2 business days   | Non-blocking decision        |
| D3       | 3 business days   | Polish / low priority        |

Do not assign D0 or D1 merely because a decision is important.

Use the actual impact on production or the current phase.

---

# DECISION STATUS FLOW

Every decision follows:

**OPEN → APPROACHING DEADLINE → ESCALATED → RESOLVED → VERIFIED**

If the decision is intentionally postponed:

**OPEN → DEFERRED → BACKLOG**

A decision marked **RESOLVED** is not automatically considered **VERIFIED**.

Verification confirms that the implementation correctly reflects the approved decision.

---

# DECISION TO IMPLEMENTATION RULE

Once a decision is resolved:

1. Record the final decision.
2. Identify affected phases/components.
3. Assign implementation owner.
4. Update the relevant specification.
5. Implement the decision.
6. Test the result.
7. Mark the decision VERIFIED.

Do not continue implementing multiple previously proposed options after a final decision has been recorded.

---

# DECISION CHANGE RULE

If an already resolved decision changes, do not overwrite the original record.

Create a new decision record.

Example:

```text
D-014
Original decision:
Use a dark hero section.

D-027
Change request:
Replace the dark hero with a light hero.

References:
Supersedes D-014.
```

The original decision remains part of the project history.

This prevents the team from losing track of why an earlier implementation existed.

---

# DECISION DEPENDENCY RULE

If a decision blocks another decision, record the dependency.

Example:

```text
D-021
Decision:
Approve project card structure.

Depends on:
D-018 — Final project categories.

Status:
WAITING ON D-018
```

Do not escalate a dependent decision as though its owner is responsible for the delay.

Resolve the upstream decision first.

---

# DECISION VS BLOCKER

A **decision** answers:

**"What should we do?"**

A **blocker** answers:

**"Why can't we continue?"**

They may be linked.

Example:

```text
DECISION:
Should the TBMS case study show reservation-management screenshots?

BLOCKER:
Verified screenshots are unavailable.

Decision ID:
D-031

Blocker ID:
B-009
```

The decision may be resolved before the blocker is resolved.

Do not mark the implementation complete until both requirements are satisfied.

# DECISION GOVERNANCE RULE

No material decision should exist only inside:

* Chat messages
* Verbal conversations
* Temporary comments
* Design annotations
* Developer assumptions
* Untracked meeting discussions

If the decision can change scope, implementation, content, architecture or launch readiness, record it.

Final governance chain:

**Decision Required → Owner Assigned → Deadline Set → Decision Made → Specification Updated → Implementation → Verification**

This decision system works together with the blocker system:

**Decision → May create or resolve blocker → Blocker resolution → Phase gate verification**

The objective is simple:

**No important decision should be forgotten, duplicated, contradicted or implemented from an assumption.**
